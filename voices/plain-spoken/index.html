plain-spoken

Warm but direct. An expert peer explaining something clearly and then stopping. Every string below is written for Fieldnote, the shared example product, so voices can be compared line for line.

1. Voice attributes

Five attributes, each with the same idea written in voice and out of voice.

Warm but direct

Care about the reader, but do not pad sentences to seem kind. Directness is the warmth.

In voiceThis will delete 3 projects and everything in them.
Out of voiceWe're really sorry, but we just wanted to make sure you know this action might unfortunately remove some of your content!

Substantive over stylish

Say the thing, then stop. The second sentence usually serves the writer.

In voiceSaved.
Out of voiceSuccess! Your changes have been saved successfully.

Plain words for ordinary things

Use the word the reader would use, unless a longer one is genuinely more precise.

In voiceYou can't undo this.
Out of voiceThis operation is non-reversible.

Never prescriptive without cause

Instruct only when there is exactly one correct next step. Otherwise name the options.

In voiceTwo projects have the same name. Rename one, or merge them.
Out of voiceYou should rename your project to avoid confusion.

Honest about failure

Say what broke, whose fault it was, and what happens next.

In voiceWe couldn't save your note. It's still here — try again in a moment.
Out of voiceSomething went wrong.

2. Style rules

Decidable without judgement. A rule you have to interpret is a preference.

RuleDecision
Headings, buttons, labels, navSentence case. Only proper nouns and acronyms capitalised.
VoiceActive. We couldn't save your note, not your note could not be saved.
TensePresent.
PersonYou for the reader. We only when the product acted or failed. Never I.
ContractionsYes — can't, don't, we'll.
Serial commaAlways.
NumbersNumerals everywhere, including 1–9. UI is scanned, not read.
DashesEm dash, unspaced. Never --, never a spaced hyphen.
Quotation marksCurly. Commas and periods inside, US English.
Terminal punctuationSentences take a period. Fragments, labels, buttons and list items do not.
Exclamation marksNone. Not one.
EmojiNone in product UI.

5. Labels, help text, placeholders

The label names the field. The help text explains format or consequence. Neither repeats the other.

Field with help text Project name Collaborators see this name
Field with format help Retention period Notes are deleted after this many days. Leave empty to keep them indefinitely
Field with a consequence Workspace URL Changing this breaks existing links
Placeholder used correctly Email address Placeholder: rosa@example.com An example of a valid value — never an instruction, never the label again.
Required field Project name (required) The word, not an asterisk. See shared/ACCESSIBILITY.md.

Strike on sight

Delete on sight and say the thing plainly. No exceptions clause.

leverageutiliseseamless delightfulrobustpowerful intuitiveeffortlesssimply justeasyunlock empowerstreamlineoptimise elevatejourneyecosystem solutionbest-in-classworld-class cutting-edgenext-generationgame-changer

simply and just tell readers that what they are struggling with is trivial. easy promises an experience you cannot guarantee — say what it takes instead: “two steps”, “about a minute”.

17. Writing antipatterns

  • “Oops!” or “Uh oh!” — cutesy framing of a failure the reader did not find cute
  • “Something went wrong.” — true and useless; name the thing
  • “Please note that…” — always deletable, as is “Please be aware that”
  • “Simply click…” / “Just add…” — see the strike list
  • “We're excited to announce…” — spends the reader's attention on your feelings
  • “Are you sure?” as a dialog title — asks about their state of mind, not the consequence
  • “Success!” standing alone — say what succeeded
  • “Invalid input” — names the verdict, not the problem or the fix
  • Hedging stacks — “you may want to consider possibly”
  • Rhetorical questions as headings — “Ready to get started?”

Length budgets

A design can rely on these when sizing a region: button ≤ 20 characters, field label ≤ 30, toast ≤ 40, page title ≤ 40, empty-state heading ≤ 40, tooltip ≤ 60, notification subject ≤ 60, help text ≤ 90, error ≤ 120, empty-state body ≤ 140. plain-spoken runs short, which makes it safe in dense designs.
